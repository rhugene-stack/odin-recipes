Sinigang na Baboy Recipe

Description

A classic Filipino sour soup that features tender cuts of pork simmered in a vibrant, savory broth infused with tamarind. It is widely considered one of the ultimate comfort foods in the Philippines, prized for its refreshing sourness (asim) that balances beautifully with the richness of the meat. The dish is traditionally loaded with a colorful assortment of local vegetables like water spinach (kang kong), taro roots (gabi), radish, and eggplant, making it a wholesome and deeply satisfying one-pot meal.

Ingredients

  • Pork Belly: 2 lbs pork belly, ribs, or shoulder, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • Tamarind soup base mix (or 1 cup fresh tamarind juice): 1 pack (1.4 oz)
  • Water spinach (kang kong): 1 bunch (leaves and tender stems)
  • Radish (labanos)): 1 medium (sliced into rounds)
  • Eggplant (talong): 1 medium (sliced)
  • Okra: 4-5 pieces(whole)
  • Taro (gabi): 2-3 pieces small (peeled and quartered)
  • Onion: 1 medium (sliced)
  • Tomatoes: 2 medium (quartered)
  • Green long chilies (siling haba): 2-3 pieces
  • Water (or rice-washed water): 6-8 cups
  • Fish sauce (patis) and black pepper: To taste

Steps

  1. Sear and simmer the meat: In a large pot, combine the pork, onions, and tomatoes. Pour in the water and bring to a boil. Lower the heat, cover, and simmer for 30 to 45 minutes until the pork becomes tender. Skim off any scum that rises to the top.
  2. Cook the taro: Add the quartered taro (gabi) into the pot. Simmer for about 10 minutes, or until the taro starts to soften and slightly thicken the broth.
  3. Sour the broth: Stir in the tamarind soup base mix. Adjust the amount according to how sour you want your broth. Season with fish sauce (patis) and ground black pepper to taste.
  4. Add the hearty vegetables: Add the radish, eggplant, okra, and green long chilies. Simmer for another 5 to 7 minutes until these vegetables are cooked through but not mushy.
  5. Wilt the greens: Turn off the heat and stir in the water spinach (kang kong). Cover the pot and let the residual heat cook the greens for 1 to 2 minutes before serving hot.
